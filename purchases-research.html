🧠 The core idea

Companies collect detailed behavioural data because it gives them an advantage. The PixelPalOS version flips the direction: the individual keeps the data and uses it to make their own life cheaper, easier, calmer and more intelligible.

Key distinction: a receipt is not the purchase, and a purchase is not the shopping trip. A receipt is evidence. A purchase is a transaction. A shopping trip is an event or Memory that can link to one or more purchases.

🔬 What the research says

Several UK studies tested almost exactly this behaviour: people using smartphones to photograph receipts and record expenditure over time.

21.7 days

Average days participants used the expenditure app during a one-month study.

7.7 hours

Average delay between purchase and receipt photograph. Most were still captured within 24 hours.

31 sec

Average interaction duration in an earlier burden study. A receipt scan averaged roughly 41 seconds.

≈74%

Receipt items correctly matched by an automated exact-string categorisation approach in one study. Human correction still mattered.

The central finding is important: receipt scanning alone under-records spending. Manual/direct entry improves coverage. PixelPalOS therefore must not let “no receipt” become “no purchase”.

Approximate truth sustained for years is more useful than perfect capture sustained for a fortnight.

Diary fatigue appears even when individual interactions are short. The burden is remembering to perform a tiny chore repeatedly. Broader personal-informatics research shows that lapsing, restarting, changing goals and abandoning trackers are normal. A robust system expects that.

🧻 The real enemy: friction

The dangerous moment is not the first missed receipt. It is the thought afterwards: “The data isn't complete now anyway, so why bother?”

Good: “August purchase coverage: about 94%. One transaction may be missing item details.”
Bad: “43-day receipt streak broken.”

There should be a graceful capture ladder: receipt photo; electronic receipt/order; recognised transaction; product photo/barcode; quick manual purchase; unitemised transaction; or nothing. The system survives every rung.

The reward for logging should be immediate usefulness, not arbitrary XP. After a scan, tell the user something worth knowing: basket versus usual spend, historical-low prices, unusual price-per-unit, probable replenishment horizon, or a genuinely new product.

Review uncertainty, not everything

If 38 of 41 Aldi lines are recognised confidently, do not make the human inspect 41 lines. Surface the three ambiguous ones. Human attention is the scarce resource.

Store evidence, not just conclusions

For inferred fields, preserve source and confidence. Years later, PixelPalOS should know the difference between something the user confirmed and something a 2026 AI guessed.

product_name = "KitKat Chunky Multipack"
source = "user_confirmed_mapping"
confidence = 1.0

shopping_duration = 25
source = "user_estimate"
confidence = 0.8

Keep the original receipt, raw extraction and parser version so old evidence can be reprocessed by better future models.

Product identity

Barcode/GTIN should be strongest when available, then retailer SKU/product code, previously confirmed receipt text, exact/fuzzy name matching and AI interpretation. Correct a mapping once and the local catalogue should learn it.

Backups

Do not stuff images into giant Base64 JSON unless there is a specific need. Keep structured data and media separately, then package them together for Full Backup.

🎒 Inventory: RPG mechanics with a practical job

Repeated purchases create a probabilistic picture of household consumables. Toilet roll, kitchen paper, freezer bags, olive oil, cat litter and cleaning products can each develop an estimated depletion state.

🧻 Toilet roll

Estimated: Low

Usually rebought every 24–31 days. Last purchase 27 days ago.

🫒 Olive oil

Estimated: Healthy

Typical interval suggests no action needed.

🧊 Freezer bags

Estimated: Overstocked-ish

Six packs purchased recently. Please stop buying freezer bags. 😄

The app should not claim exact stock from purchase frequency alone. Reordering before zero, stockpiling, guests and unusual consumption all create uncertainty. The “health bar” therefore represents estimated depletion or probability of needing more, not a fictional exact count.

That is still enough to support useful prompts such as “82% chance you'll want toilet roll within the next week”.

📦 Ownership: purchases that continue to exist

A purchase is a transaction. An Asset is a thing that continues afterwards. Durable purchases should be able to create Assets automatically or with one confirmation.

Purchase: £799 television → retailer → 14 Nov 2026
Asset: LG OLED TV → Living room → Owned → warranty until 14 Nov 2031

An Asset can accumulate model, serial number, photos, receipt, manual, warranty, manufacturer, retailer, room/location, repairs, maintenance, accessories and eventual sale/disposal.

This is useful for insurance because household-inventory guidance recommends maintaining records of belongings and evidence such as receipts, invoices and photographs. PixelPalOS can bootstrap that inventory naturally from buying things rather than demanding a separate cataloguing project.

🫧 Hot tub example

The Asset could hold the original purchase, supplier contact, manual PDF, filter type, replacement parts, warranty, maintenance events and repair history. “What filter does the hot tub use?” becomes a query against your own records.

Returns should not erase history

A returned TV remains a purchase event plus a linked refund and an Asset whose status becomes returned. That preserves what actually happened and keeps financial analysis honest.
